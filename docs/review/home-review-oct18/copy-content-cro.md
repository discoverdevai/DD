# Home page review: copy, content, proof, CRO (Oct 18, 2026)

Scope: built `dist/index.html` text, `home.ts`, `projects.ts`, checked against 09, 03 §2A, 02, 01, 08, 11. Read-only.

## 1. Verdict

The page is honest, plain and on-voice. Nearly every claim traces to 09, and there is no invented proof, price hype or jargon wall (framework names stay off home). The weaknesses are strategic. The promise targets only idea-stage founders. The same four ideas ("before any code", senior sign-off, weekly demo, no handoff gaps) repeat 15+ times. Proof comes after five explanation sections, and the reviews are short and generic. The senior team is never shown, and visitors learn neither what the call is nor what anything costs. One factual error is live: "GMT+2" is wrong in Egypt until Oct 29. The headline promise "idea to live in 8–12 weeks" also leaves out Validate.

## 4. Voice and AI-tell check

- Exclamation marks only in two verbatim quotes (Michelle, Lea). Acceptable. No em-dashes in body.
- Contrast reveals: "AI cost follows unique questions, not total requests"; "speed never costs you quality"; "Most teams just build what you ask for. We help you build the right thing" (mandated core idea; keep it in Why only).
- Stock or jingle lines: "AI does the heavy lifting", "speed without the mess", "Senior-led, AI-fast", "The 'Discover' in our name is the point", "One team, no handoff gaps".
- Repetition counts: "before any code" 6, weekly demo or progress 5, "no pitch" 5, "every release" 4, "handoff" 3, "senior" about 25.

## 5. Section findings

**Hero (Medium).** Must land in 3 seconds. The H1 serves only idea founders; growing teams, AI-built app owners and agencies get nothing until Engagements. "In weeks" then "8–12 weeks" is vague then stretched. Strengths: "You own everything", rating line, quiet line.
- Rewrite: *Turn your idea into a live product in 8–12 weeks.* Sub: *DiscoverDev is a product and engineering company. Senior engineers and AI build it, and you own everything.* Add under the buttons: *Already have an app or a team? See your options.* (anchor to Engagements). Or say "after a 1–2 week Validate".

**Proof band (Medium).** Ten named logos with descriptors are the best early trust signal; the label over-reaches. The four facts are generic and the map repeats "4 continents".
- Label: *Teams we've worked with.* Facts: *Clients on 4 continents and 9 countries* [NEED: confirm country list is complete]; drop "Arabic and English" duplicates elsewhere.

**The DiscoverDev Way (Low-Medium).** Clear body but it restates the hero board and then the four-card journey. A CTA here comes before any work is shown.
- Body: *We agree on what is worth building before any code. AI drafts, a senior engineer signs off every release, and you see progress every week.* Cut or confirm "Security, data and payments". Move the CTA after the proof.

**Lifecycle (Medium).** Overlaps Engagements with different names and identical durations ("Discover 1–2 weeks" is Validate). "Greenfield or brownfield" is jargon. Plan's time is "Before any code" and its body repeats it.
- Lead: *Starting from an idea or from code you already have, the steps are the same. With existing code, we begin by reviewing what you have.* Label steps *Discover (Validate)*, *Ship and Stay (Scale)*, or merge the two sections.

**Engagements (Low).** Best block: "I have an idea." / "I'm ready to build." / "It's working. Grow it." uses the visitor's words. No price or risk signal on the Review row.
- Add: *From $1,490. No useful findings, no charge.* (09, D-01/D-03).

**Capabilities (Medium).** 15 items fail the swap test, repeat "handoff", and interrupt the story between offers and proof.
- Cut to four one-line groups or move below the work. AI tagline: *AI agents, computer vision and data, with Arabic and English chatbots.*

**Project chapters (Medium).** Real screens are strong. NJWAR is named with an unconfirmed client link and a "Saudi Arabia" tag; chapters show no result, so they read as portfolio. Two CTAs per chapter.
- Until consent: *An AR furniture store with AI recommendations.* [NEED: backed result line; consent for "NJWAR" and the location tag.]

**Selected work (Low-Medium).** The EdTech tile with a real number is the page's best proof. Other tiles carry no outcome. Heading repeats the chapters' message.
- EdTech: *AI costs down 60–90%, depending on cache hits and caption availability. Cost now grows slower than the user base.* Heading: *More work.*

**Reviews (Medium-High).** The section the ICP needs most is the weakest. Seven of eight quotes are generic, none states what was built, how fast, or that the team stayed, and four of eight are market-research gigs. "CEO" is unnamed. The CTA after the reviews is well placed.
- Feature the EduLens quote with one result line (*Three AI agents, AI costs down 60–90%*), collapse the rest, link "Read all on Clutch". [NEED: a quote on speed, ownership or a repeat engagement.]

**Why / pillars (Medium).** All four restate the Way, lifecycle or FAQ. The unique content is "we tell you what to cut" and "Four clients expanded scope".
- Merge to three pillars. Pillar 2: *Senior engineers review every change and sign off every release. AI does the heavy lifting, so it moves fast.* Move the "Four clients" fact to the proof band.

**Guides (Low).** Serves the AI-built-app segment with low commitment; strong titles. Keep.

**FAQ (Medium).** Good questions; the AI answer over-promises; pricing gives no anchor; the time zone has the GMT error. Missing: "What happens on the call?", "Do I need to be technical?", "Will juniors work on my project?" (top objection, 02).
- AI: *AI drafts code, tests and documentation. Senior engineers decide, review every change and sign off each release.* Time zone: *Our engineering hub is in Alexandria, Egypt (Cairo time). Our working day overlaps US mornings and Europe. We work in Arabic and English.*

**Closing band and footer (Low).** "Tell us what you're building ... even if we're not the right fit" is the best CTA copy on the page. Add *30 minutes. We tell you where to start.* Footer is fine (rating shown a third time).

## 6. CRO

- **CTAs:** "Book a free clarity call" appears five times in body plus header and footer, same wording and target. About 14 secondary links (See Validate, See the Review, guides, project links) form a third tier. No competing primaries.
- **Trust placement:** rating under the hero CTA and reviews before a CTA are right. Work and reviews start at about 60% depth; add a one-line proof (EduLens 60–90% + rating) near the hero.
- **Friction:** a call is the only conversion for idea and growth leads. [NEED: a lighter first step for idea founders; D-11 allows a form to hello@.]
- **Missing vs competitors (08):** visible people (D-16/D-17 hold it), a price anchor (rescue shops publish; $1,490 is unused on home), a timeline benchmark (TrianglZ publishes MVP 4–8 weeks; 09's "vs typical 12–16" is unused), the guarantee near a CTA.
- **Objections:** ownership, AI safety, time zones handled; "offshore means juniors", cost, and "what happens on the call" thin.

## 7. Gaps and duplication
