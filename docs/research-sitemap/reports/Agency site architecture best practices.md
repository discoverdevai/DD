# Small agencies win trust with fewer, deeper pages

DiscoverDev's current plan (home, three services, how-we-work, work, about, a Saudi page, guides, Arabic twins) already matches what mid-size agencies like Monterail, Railsware and thoughtbot ship, so the plan needs sharpening more than expanding. The evidence says the page types that answer "how do we buy, what will it cost, how will it run, can I trust you" are rare and are where the few agencies that publish them stand out: only **2 of 15 agencies checked publish a pricing page** (Creole Studios, Halo Lab), and none had comparison pages, glossaries or a dated cost guide ([agency inventory, Oct 26 2026](https://halo-lab.com/pricing)). Google's spam policies argue against broad city or keyword page grids, and no study supports page count as a ranking lever, so a small site should cap pages by how much unique proof each can carry. For the Gulf, the closest analogue is Creole Studios' Arabic Saudi page, which declares a named local partner instead of faking an office ([Creole Studios KSA](https://www.creolestudios.com/ksa/ar/)). The top additions are a pricing and "how budgets work" page with a stated floor, a security and trust page, a dated English and native-Arabic cost guide, a no-gate estimator, and a plainly worded "how we work in Saudi Arabia" section. Evidence quality is weak throughout: the research used model-summarised page fetches and search summaries, most statistics are vendor claims, and there is no conversion data tying any page type to leads.

## Leading agencies run small navs, and the differentiators sit off the nav

Fifteen agency sites were opened (Metalab, Fantasy and Distillery returned almost nothing, and one "Vention" result was an unrelated automation company). Nav is almost always 5-7 items: Services, Industries, Work, Resources, About, one CTA. Monterail has six top items and no dropdowns ([Monterail](https://www.monterail.com)); thoughtbot has six ([thoughtbot](https://thoughtbot.com)); large vendors such as Appinventiv (about 30 industry links) and STX Next (8 service groups, 14 solution pages) use mega-menus ([Appinventiv](https://appinventiv.com), [STX Next](https://www.stxnext.com)). Breadth beyond six items is a large-agency SEO pattern, not a buyer need. Programmatic pages (Toptal's skill pages, Stormotion's ~20 niche pages, Ramotion's city pages) sit in footers, not navigation ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com), [Stormotion](https://stormotion.io), [Ramotion](https://www.ramotion.com)).

Case studies, service pages, blog, about, careers and contact are near universal. The rarer, higher-value pieces are packaged offers (thoughtbot's Shaping Sprint, Railsware's Tech Audit, Ramotion's fixed-name programs), process assets (10Clouds' how-we-work method page, Railsware's BRIDGeS framework and Handbook), and security pages (Appinventiv, Ramotion's trust portal) ([thoughtbot](https://thoughtbot.com), [10Clouds](https://10clouds.com), [Railsware](https://railsware.com)). AI-built-app rescue ("Fix Vibe Code") now appears as a service at both Railsware and Intellectsoft ([Intellectsoft](https://www.intellectsoft.net)). For case studies, the best parts are spread across sites: thoughtbot leads index cards with a headline metric, Netguru offers filters by service, industry, country and tech with roughly 190 studies, and Railsware and Itransition show team size, timeline and stack ([thoughtbot case studies](https://thoughtbot.com/case-studies), [Netguru clients](https://www.netguru.com/clients), [Railsware Calendly](https://railsware.com/case-studies/calendly/)). Many detail pages lack outcome numbers (Monterail, Railsware), so a quantified result is itself a differentiator. DiscoverDev should note this applies only to proof cleared in `context/09-proof.md`.

On pricing, the two models differ. Creole publishes sprint and role rates (Discovery team $3,000 per sprint, MVP team $7,500) ([Creole pricing](https://www.creolestudios.com/our-pricing/)). Halo Lab publishes no rate card but states a **$5,000 minimum**, explains price drivers, shows a comparison against freelancers and enterprise providers, and tags testimonials by budget range ([Halo Lab pricing](https://halo-lab.com/pricing)). The Halo Lab pattern fits a small senior-led shop better, because it commits to a floor without a rate card. Demand for transparency is real but old: a 2020 Clutch survey of 451 buyers found 25% ranked cost information the most important website element ([Clutch via Indrastra](https://www.indrastra.com/2020/02/Clutch-s-Survey-B2B-Buyers-Concern-Cost-Transparency-006-02-2020-0044.html)). The counterpoint is also in the evidence: one agency analysis found hidden pricing yields more top-of-funnel leads (4.6% vs 2.8%) though quality shifts down-funnel ([Eight25 Media](https://www.eight25media.com/blog/transparent-vs-non-transparent-pricing-the-real-impact-on-sales)). Published MVP ranges disagree two- to threefold (Netguru $15k-$150k, Designli $10k-$400k+ by tier), so any range needs a stated scope ([Netguru](https://www.netguru.com/blog/mvp-costs), [Designli](https://designli.co/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-an-mvp)).

## Search and AI citation reward dated, answer-first pages and third-party mentions, not new files

Google says the basics suffice for AI Overviews and AI Mode: crawlable pages, internal links, text content, snippet eligibility, and "no special schema.org structured data" is required ([Google AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Its spam policies define doorway abuse as pages targeted at specific regions that funnel users to one page, and scaled content abuse as many pages made to manipulate rankings, however generated ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). The implication for a Gulf plan is one page per country with real proof, never a city by service grid.

Citation studies are all vendor-run and correlational. The Wix/Peec study (1.06M citations across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Perplexity) found listicles at **21.9%** of citations overall and **40.9%** of commercial-intent citations, articles at 45.5% of informational ones, comparison pages at 2.2% ([Wix AI Search Lab](https://www.wix.com/studio/ai-search-lab/research/content-types-most-cited-by-llms)). Ahrefs found 76.1% of AI Overview citations came from top-10 pages, but overlap with Google's top 10 for assistants like ChatGPT averages only about 11%, and brand mentions correlate with AI visibility (YouTube mentions ~0.74) ([Ahrefs AIO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10), [Ahrefs brand study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations)). A 100-prompt B2B check found one third-party site (TechRadar) dominating citations ([DataDab](https://www.datadab.com/research/what-chatgpt-actually-cites)). Reading these together: for "best software development company" queries, third-party lists and directories get cited, so DiscoverDev's job is inclusion there (Clutch, roundups, LinkedIn, YouTube) plus owning its own cost, process and comparison content. This is inference; no study covered agency queries. Freshness data points the same way: ChatGPT favours newer pages, and half of pages it cited were updated within three months ([Ahrefs freshness](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/), summary only).

On technical items, the evidence is clear enough to settle priorities. llms.txt showed **no correlation with citation** across about 300,000 domains, and Google says no AI text files are needed ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/llms-txt/)); it is harmless but not worth effort. Schema evidence conflicts: an AirOps correlation shows JSON-LD pages cited more (38.5% vs 32.0%), while an Ahrefs live test found assistants read only visible HTML ([Analyzify summary](https://analyzify.com/hub/schema-markup-ai-citations-research), [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)). Keep Organization, Service, Article and BreadcrumbList for Google and entity clarity, make every claim visible in text, and skip FAQ rich-result expectations since Google removed them in 2026 ([Google FAQPage doc](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage)). Allow search bots in robots.txt, because blocking them can remove a site from AI answers (third-party claim; Anthropic documents ClaudeBot opt-out at [Anthropic support](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8896518)).

## Content that fills real gaps: dated cost guides, rescue, and decision pages

The English "MVP cost" format is saturated: Netguru, Stormotion, Agilie, Designli, Yellow and others rank in search summaries, yet the sampled Netguru page showed a last-updated date of April 2025, no table, no calculator and an internal inconsistency ($10k vs $15k minimum) ([Netguru](https://www.netguru.com/blog/mvp-costs)). Most agency "calculators" are email-capture forms, so an instant no-gate estimate is the upgrade ([Smol Launch note](https://echai.ventures/startingup/from/smol-launch)). The calculator uplift claims (VenturePact "41% conversion", "+125% lead quality") are unverified vendor numbers and should not drive the decision. In Arabic, Origami's June 2026 Saudi cost page is the strongest competitor: about 1,400 words, a tier table (simple 15-30k SAR, mid 30-80k SAR, complex 80-250k+ SAR), a worked 60k SAR example, five sourced references, a 5-question FAQ, and WhatsApp plus booking hand-off, with no update date and an "AI-assisted" disclosure ([Origami](https://origami.sa/blog/mobile-app-development-cost-saudi-arabia/)). That is a beatable bar with a dated, human-authored, SAR and AED page including Arabic and RTL cost, mada and STC Pay line items.

AI-built-app rescue is crowded on supply (Empat, DBB, Managed Code and others), and Empat's page publishes $15k-80k over 4-10 weeks with a $5-10k fixed-scope opener and a written findings report in five business days ([Empat](https://www.empat.tech/blog/vibecode-rescue)). Demand is unproven: one skeptic found no public billed rescue case and says non-technical founders budget under $2k ([Rentier Digital](https://rentierdigital.xyz/blog/vibe-code-rescue-market-analysis)). No Arabic rescue content surfaced. The sensible move is one page with a small fixed-scope audit, not a content cluster. Comparison pages (freelancer vs agency vs in-house, offshore vs nearshore) exist but are generic and vendor-biased; no strong fixed-price vs time-and-materials page and no Saudi-specific offshore guide covering PDPL or data residency surfaced ([Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/resources/tips/app-development-company-vs-freelancer-vs-in-house-team), [EngineerBabu](https://engineerbabu.com/blog/offshore-vs-nearshore-vs-onshore-software-development/)). HubSpot's State of AEO reportedly puts comparison content at 95% for ChatGPT, but that is a search summary of an unopened source ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/content-format-types-that-earn-citations)). Glossaries have weak, vendor-only support; treat 5-10 entries as internal-link infrastructure. Self-published SEO case studies (+100% to +261% traffic) are anecdote.

## Trust pages and conversion practice favour specifics over volume

Buyers check IP ownership, repo access, NDA, data handling and certifications. Dedicated trust pages exist at Codersera (states no SOC 2 or ISO, works in client cloud and repos, mutual NDA, IP transfer on payment), Kitemetric (no certification, synthetic staging data) and Eastgate (ISO 27001 with registration number) ([Codersera](https://codersera.com/security), [Kitemetric](https://kitemetric.com/security.html), [Eastgate](https://eastgate-software.com/trust/security/)). The honest "no certificate, here is what we do" version is already an accepted pattern. Specific proof beats generic proof: a 2020 Clutch survey found 94% of buyers use reviews and most read them on company websites (68%) ([Clutch via Indrastra](https://www.indrastra.com/2020/02/Clutch-s-Survey-B2B-Buyers-Concern-Cost-Transparency-006-02-2020-0044.html)), and anonymous quotes read as near worthless ([Webolutions](https://webolutionsmarketingagency.com/lead-generation-strategy/conversion-optimization/b2b-website-trust-signals/)). This agrees with the project rule against invented proof.

Conversion benchmarks are directional: agencies average about 3.3% site-to-lead, with top performers near 13.5% ([Ruler Analytics](https://www.ruleranalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/Lead-conversion-rates-by-industry.pdf)), and desktop converts roughly 2x mobile in several secondary sources ([BMG360](https://www.bmg360.com/blog/post/benchmarking-mobile-vs-desktop-conversion-rates-for-15-ecommerce-and-lead-gen-brands)). No source offered a visit-to-booked-call rate for dev agencies or a controlled test of "Book a call" against "Get a quote". Forms with 3-5 fields outperform longer ones in reported Unbounce/HubSpot data, though primary studies were not reached ([Impact+](https://impactplus.com/blog/reasons-your-forms-arent-converting)). Instant-scheduling uplifts (30% to 67%) come from Chili Piper SaaS demo stories and may not transfer to a high-ticket engagement ([Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/customer-story/saasoptics)). The defensible approach is one primary booking CTA plus a lower-commitment path (short message or WhatsApp), measured on qualified leads.

## Gulf and Arabic: a country page, a stated partner model, native Arabic, and no unearned claims

Saudi-facing vendors publish a country landing page in both languages with separate URL trees (Creole at `/ksa/ar/`; Appinventiv at `/sa/` and `/ar/`), lead with certifications and named integrations (Nafath, Absher, Mada, Sadad, ZATCA), and mostly self-report ([Appinventiv KSA](https://appinventiv.com/sa/software-development-company-in-saudi-arabia/), [Mozn](https://www.mozn.ai/), [Tamkeen](https://www.tamkeentech.sa/)). Creole's page runs 15 blocks including an 8-question FAQ, cities, partner section and cost CTA, and relies on a named local partner (Surnouf Technology) with 24/7 local support; it also shows placeholder client logos, a pattern to avoid ([Creole Studios KSA](https://www.creolestudios.com/ksa/ar/)). Another Riyadh-branded vendor advertises ISO 27001 while delivering from India, and buyers are advised to demand certificate numbers and Saudi references ([CloudHouse](https://cloudhousetechnologies.com/services/mobile-app-development-company-riyadh)). A smaller but verifiable list therefore stands out.

On buyer rules, government bidding via Etimad requires a Saudi commercial registration, VAT registration, bank account and related papers ([SetupInSaudi](https://www.setupinsaudi.com/en/news/announcements/how-to-use-saudi-arabia-s-etimad-to-bid-on-governmental-contracts)); the regional-HQ rule bars state entities from contracting with foreign firms whose regional HQ is outside the Kingdom, with exceptions and a reported exemption for projects of SAR 1 million or less ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2633989/), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fnew-rule-saudi-arabia-reverses-restriction-allows-foreign-companies-in-government-projects-1.500451195)). Sources disagree on how strictly private buyers require local entities. The PDPL, amended in 2023, requires "appropriate level of protection" for transfers abroad and SDAIA standard contractual clauses ([Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/saudi-arabia-new-amendments-have-been-introduced-to-the-personal-data-protection-law)). ZATCA e-invoicing waves target Saudi VAT-registered businesses, so it is likely a partner's concern rather than an Egyptian firm's, though that is inference needing legal confirmation ([EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1705-saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration)). The practical reading: sell directly to startups and private buyers, route anything government-adjacent through the partner, never claim NCA compliance or certificates not held, and phrase PDPL, Nafath, mada and ZATCA as "we integrate with".

For Arabic SEO, Google accepts language-only hreflang (`ar`), so use `en` and `ar` plus `x-default`, adding `ar-SA` and `en-SA` only on a Saudi page pair with distinct content ([Google localized versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)). Arabic keyword volumes are low, practitioners lean on SERP analysis, and MSA is the safe baseline for B2B ([Argos Multilingual](https://www.argosmultilingual.com/blog/arabic-seo)). For UX, `dir="rtl"` does not mirror directional icons, Arabic needs more line-height and zero letter-spacing, and phone numbers and Latin tech terms stay LTR ([Saudisoft RTL checklist](https://localization.saudisoft.com/?p=38918)). The existing logical-properties rule matches this. The only channel evidence for WhatsApp is consumer-side: 85% of UAE residents would prefer it for business contact (YouGov for Zbooni, vendor-commissioned) ([Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/85-percent-of-uae-residents-want-businesses-to-use-whatsapp/)), and no B2B or Saudi data was found.

## Evidence quality and gaps

Strong evidence: Google's own policy documents (spam, AI features, hreflang, FAQ deprecation) and the observed contents of agency pages. Moderate: the Wix/Peec, Ahrefs and SE Ranking studies, which are large but vendor-run and correlational, and the law-firm summaries of PDPL. Weak: nearly all conversion statistics, which come from vendor blogs and second-hand citations (the Forrester 27% and Gartner 71% pricing claims were unverified; the Clutch survey is from 2020), and the calculator, rescue-demand and WhatsApp claims. Absence claims ("no agency has a glossary") rest on nav and footer text from a summarising fetch tool, not a crawl; no sitemap.xml was parsed. The Gulf pass opened only five vendor sites; Elm and Thiqah failed to load; no Kuwait or UAE agency pages were read; nothing verifies Arabic and English parity on Mozn or Tamkeen, Arabic title-length limits, dialect effects, Arabizi queries, or B2B tone norms. No keyword volumes or SERP positions were retrieved for any query (no SEO tool access), so every "gap" judgment about Arabic or rescue queries is unverified until checked in Search Console or Ahrefs. NCA requirements were not read from a primary source.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "what pages do agencies have" to "which buyer questions do almost no agencies answer in public." Pricing, security, a dated cost guide and an honest account of local delivery are the open spaces; page count, llms.txt and clever schema are not. For DiscoverDev the Gulf angle works only if it is specific and checkable, which means a short list of real clients, a named partner with consent, and integration claims phrased as work done rather than credentials held. That is also the easier route under the project's no-invented-proof rule.

Before building the Arabic cost guide or the rescue page, spend an hour in Search Console and Ahrefs checking Arabic and English volumes and top results, since every demand judgment above rests on search summaries rather than SERP data. Get legal confirmation on the contracting and invoicing model, and treat the first six months of any conversion-oriented change as a measured test rather than a known win.
