# Agency site architecture: page inventory and navigation patterns (checked Oct 26, 2026)

Method note: each site's live homepage or inner page was fetched on 2026-10-26 via WebFetch, which returns a model-summarised view of the page. Nav menus can be truncated or reflect only what rendered in the fetched HTML. Where a fetch gave no footer or nav, it is flagged. 15 of 18 named agencies were opened with usable data; Metalab, Fantasy and Distillery returned little (see Gaps). No sitemap.xml could be parsed (Netguru's exceeded 10 MB).

## 1. Top navigation, service page set, proof sections per agency

### Takeaway
Nav is almost always 5-7 items: Services, Industries, Work/Case studies, Resources/Insights/Blog, About/Company, one CTA. Mid-size agencies (thoughtbot, Railsware, Monterail, Halo Lab) keep it flat and small; large ones (Netguru, Itransition, Appinventiv, STX Next) use mega-menus with 40-100+ links. Case studies are near-universal; pricing, how-we-work and security pages are rare.

## 2. Recurring vs rare page types

### Takeaway
Recurring (nearly all 15 sites): service pages, case studies/portfolio, blog, about, careers, contact, legal. Common in larger/mid sites: industry pages (13 of 15 checked). Rare but high-value: public pricing page, how-we-work/process page, security/compliance page, handbook/open playbook, proprietary framework, in-house research report, packaged productised offers.

### Cited Findings
- **Industries**: thoughtbot, Netguru, Intellectsoft, Itransition, Appinventiv (~30), Creole, Halo Lab, 10Clouds (sectors), STX Next (11), Stormotion (expertise niches). Not in Monterail, Railsware (only Fintech and AI case-study links), Ramotion (industry branding pages in footer only). — links under Section 1.
- **Location/city pages**: Ramotion (SF, LA, NYC at /digital-product-agency-san-francisco/ etc.) — [ramotion.com](https://www.ramotion.com); Stormotion regional pages (/app-development-australia/, /app-development-company-usa/) — [stormotion.io](https://stormotion.io); Creole "Area We Serve" (/area-we-serve/) — [creolestudios.com](https://www.creolestudios.com).
- **Pricing**: Creole Studios /our-pricing/ and Halo Lab /pricing exist (details in Section 4). Appinventiv has cost guides instead of a pricing page. Not found on thoughtbot, Netguru, Intellectsoft, Itransition, Railsware, 10Clouds, STX Next, Monterail, Stormotion, Ramotion.
- **Engagement models**: Netguru nav (Dedicated Teams, Staff Augmentation, Delivery Center) — [netguru.com](https://www.netguru.com); Intellectsoft footer group "Engagement Models" — [intellectsoft.net](https://www.intellectsoft.net); Creole lists Project Based, Dedicated Team, Time & Material, Product Engineering — [creolestudios.com/our-pricing](https://www.creolestudios.com/our-pricing/); Halo Lab has /services/dedicated-team in the main nav; thoughtbot has team-augmentation and fractional-leadership service pages.
- **How we work / process**: 10Clouds /how-we-work/ + /how-we-work/10c-agile-product-delivery-method/ (four-stage approach: diagnose and prioritise, configure and integrate, validate, pilot and operate) — [10clouds.com](https://10clouds.com); Appinventiv /how-we-work/ — [appinventiv.com](https://appinventiv.com); Ramotion /process/ — [ramotion.com](https://www.ramotion.com); Railsware BRIDGeS framework (7 pages) and Handbook — [railsware.com](https://railsware.com); thoughtbot Playbook (external) — [thoughtbot.com](https://thoughtbot.com). None found at STX Next, Monterail (process described in a blog article only).
- **Security / compliance / trust**: Appinventiv /security/, /compliances/, /faq/, /awards/ in About menu — [appinventiv.com](https://appinventiv.com); Ramotion Trust Portal (external) and /reviews/ — [ramotion.com](https://www.ramotion.com); Intellectsoft /anti-fraud-statement; 10Clouds /whistleblowing/; Netguru B-Corp/TÜV NORD badges; thoughtbot /accessibility-statement and /conduct; STX Next /code-of-conduct.
- **Glossary**: none found on any of the 15 sites' nav/footer.
- **Comparison pages**: none found on any agency (Ramotion/Halo Lab have service-vs-self comparison only inside the Halo Lab pricing page table). Toptal programmatic "skill" pages are the nearest analogue.
- **Partners**: STX Next /our-partners/{aws,azure,snowflake,databricks}; Netguru footer partners (Microsoft, AWS, VTEX, Webflow).
- **Careers**: present on all (thoughtbot /jobs, Netguru /join-netguru, Intellectsoft /careers, STX Next external, Monterail /careers, Stormotion /careers, Railsware /careers).
- **Own products / open source / podcasts as authority**: Railsware products (Mailtrap, Coupler.io), thoughtbot open source/podcasts/books, Monterail The New Default podcast, Netguru podcast and newsletters, Halo Lab /opensource, STX Next CTO Survey.
- **Packaged/productised offers**: thoughtbot Shaping Sprints (/services/shaping-sprint), Railsware Tech Audit, Itransition assessment page, STX Next "Accelerators", 10Clouds Credit Process / Agentic Commerce, Ramotion Programs (fixed-scope redesign/discovery/delivery).
- **Referral program**: Railsware /referral-program/, Halo Lab /referral-program.

### Inferences
- Pages that answer "how do we buy and what will it cost/how will it run" (pricing, engagement, process, security) are the least common and are where the few agencies that have them differentiate.
- Programmatic niche pages (Stormotion ~20 niche pages, Toptal skills, Creole "hire X", Ramotion city pages) are an SEO tactic rather than a navigation feature: they sit in footers, not top nav.

### Gaps
- Without a crawled sitemap, counts of pages per site are not verified.
- Absence claims ("not found") are based on nav/footer as returned by the fetch tool, not a full site crawl.

## 3. Case study structure and volume

### Takeaway
Structures vary from short narrative (Railsware ~500 words, Monterail) to formal Challenge/Solution/Services/Technologies/Process/Results (Itransition). Quantified outcomes are common on index cards and homepages but patchy inside the case study body. Volume ranges from ~12 (thoughtbot) to ~190 (Netguru, 48 cards x ~4 pages).

### Cited Findings
- **thoughtbot**: 12 unique case studies on /case-studies, no filters; "featured results" cards lead with a headline metric (e.g. "0 to 4M users in months"); story cards show thumbnail, category label (e.g. Ruby on Rails), title, one-line summary. Detail page (CloseKnit Health): Title, metadata (Client, Services, Description), The Result, problem as question, What We Did, closing CTA; quantified: 8-10x release speed over a year, with quote from client product manager (page also states "10x", slight inconsistency). — [index](https://thoughtbot.com/case-studies), [detail](https://thoughtbot.com/case-studies/closeknit-health)
- **Netguru**: /clients has 48 cards on page 1 with about 4 pages (roughly 190 total, inferred from pagination), with filters: Services (12), Industries (19), Countries (24), Company size (3), Tech stack (16). Cards: image, optional logo, category tag, title, 1-2 sentence summary. URL: /clients/{slug}. Homepage quantified claims: 40% growth in international/GCC orders, 21% higher conversion, 60% more user engagement, 5.0 App Store rating; Silk Design System card title "50% Efficiency Gains". — [netguru.com/clients](https://www.netguru.com/clients), [netguru.com](https://www.netguru.com)
- **Itransition** detail page (stock trading platform): Challenge, Solution, Services, Technologies, Process, Results. Results quantified (QA 98-100% test pass; server provisioning 20x faster; 80+ screener parameters), very large tech list, team composition by role, no client quote. — [itransition.com/portfolio/stock-trading-software-development](https://www.itransition.com/portfolio/stock-trading-software-development)
- **Railsware** (Calendly): About, Involvement, Technologies, Integrations; details block with client location, team (22 people: 17 engineers, 2 designers, 1 PM, 1 EM, 1 QA), timeline (Jan 2014 to present), founder quote and video; few numeric outcomes (about 2-3 minutes saved per appointment). Nav surfaces named logos (Calendly, Tradezella). — [railsware.com/case-studies/calendly](https://railsware.com/case-studies/calendly/)
- **Monterail** (Merck Unite): overview, company and product, Client's feedback, Scope and Highlights, The Challenge, The Process, Team's voice, The Outcome, "Read more case studies"; named client quote and tech stack (Rails API, Angular, PostgreSQL), team of 11; no outcome metrics. Index at /projects. — [monterail.com/projects/merck-unite](https://www.monterail.com/projects/merck-unite)
- **Ramotion**: 14+ case studies at root slugs, named brands (Firefox, Descript, Clearbit, Cellebrite, Flatfile) — [ramotion.com](https://www.ramotion.com). **Appinventiv** portfolio with household brands (Adidas, KFC, IKEA, Domino's) — [appinventiv.com](https://appinventiv.com). **Intellectsoft** 7 case URLs at /cases/{slug} seen. **Stormotion** /portfolio/{client}. **Creole** /our-works/{slug}. **Halo Lab** /project/{slug}. **STX Next** /case-study/{slug} plus /portfolio with references.
- **Fantasy** case study block: "Case study" label, client name (LIV Golf), summary statement — [fantasy.co](https://fantasy.co).
- **Vention** (not an agency): five customer stories on homepage and aggregate proof (4.7x ROI average) — [vention.com](https://vention.com/).

### Inferences
- Best pattern seen: metric on the index card (thoughtbot), filter facets by service/industry/tech/size/country (Netguru), team composition + timeline + tech stack in a details block (Railsware, Itransition). No single site combines all.
- Many detail pages lack outcome metrics (Monterail, Railsware), so quantified results are a differentiator.

### Gaps
- Distillery, Metalab full case studies not opened. Exact total case-study counts for most sites not verified.
- Third-party 2025-26 teardown articles on agency case studies were not found in search (only generic "essential pages" listicles).

## 4. Pricing, "how we work", engagement-model and comparison pages

### Takeaway
Only 2 of 15 agencies checked publish a standalone pricing page (Creole Studios, Halo Lab), built in very different ways. Most others replace pricing with engagement-model, process, or cost-guide content. No standalone comparison pages found.

### Cited Findings
- **Creole Studios /our-pricing/**: hero "Flexible Pricing Model & 48 hours Deployment Time"; value tiles; two models, "Hire Team" and "On Demand"; 4-step sprint process (Scope Definition, Sprint Division, Sprint Execution, Sprint Invoice, two-week sprints); published prices: Discovery team $3,000/sprint, Development team $6,000/sprint, MVP team $7,500/sprint (marked recommended); individual roles from about $2k-$2.8k/month and $18-$22/hour, PM/Scrum Master/cloud/GenAI/ML from $3.5k/month and $30/hour; hiring process (Inquiry, Interview, Free Trial, NDA & Contract). Page has no FAQ and states conflicting trial lengths (3 days banner vs "24 Horus" typo in process). — [creolestudios.com/our-pricing](https://www.creolestudios.com/our-pricing/). Also a separate Software Development Cost Calculator at /software-development-cost-calculator/.
- **Halo Lab /pricing** (footer label "Calculator"): no rate card. Sections: three budget pain points with stats, comparison table versus freelancers and enterprise providers (qualitative ratings), social proof (500+ projects, 4.9 avg), price drivers (complexity, speed, quality; platforms, technical complexity, features, custom design), 5-step process starting with a free estimate via smart calculator, testimonials tagged with budget ranges ($10-50K, $50-200K), CEO note, FAQ ($5,000 minimum, why no fixed price list, example ranges about $5,000 to $200,000+ citing 2022, payment models), booking and estimate forms with budget select ($10,000 to $200,000+). — [halo-lab.com/pricing](https://halo-lab.com/pricing). Contact form budget tiers also shown on the homepage.
- **Appinventiv /guide/mobile-app-development-cost/**: very long guide (10 numbered sections) giving ranges ($40,000 to $400,000+ across basic/moderate/advanced/enterprise), cost drivers, hidden costs, per-stage costs, example apps, regional hourly rates, AI cost effect, FAQs, and a multi-step "Request a Custom Quote" form; no interactive calculator. — [appinventiv.com guide](https://appinventiv.com/guide/mobile-app-development-cost/)
- **Distillery**: third-party Clutch listing shows min project $25,000+, $50-$99/hour; not published on its own site (as far as could be verified). — [search result](https://www.clutch.co/profile/distillery?page=1) (Clutch page not opened directly).
- **Engagement-model pages**: Netguru (Dedicated Teams, Staff Augmentation, Delivery Center in Services nav), Intellectsoft (/services/dedicated-development-team, footer group), thoughtbot (team augmentation, fractional leadership), Halo Lab (/services/dedicated-team), Ramotion programs (Redesign / Discovery / Delivery with fixed names).
- **How-we-work**: see Section 2 (10Clouds, Appinventiv, Ramotion /process/, Railsware BRIDGeS and Handbook).
- **Comparison/alternatives pages**: none found among the 15 sites' navs. Halo Lab embeds a "us versus freelancers/enterprise" table in the pricing page.

### Inferences
- Two pricing styles: published sprint/role rates (Creole) vs explanation-of-drivers plus minimum budget and example ranges (Halo Lab). The Halo Lab pattern states a floor and honest ranges without a rate card and pairs them with budget-tagged testimonials, which is the more transferable pattern for a small senior-led shop.
- Cost-guide articles (Appinventiv) capture search demand for "how much does X cost" without committing to quotes.

### Gaps
- Creole's rate figures are listed on the page; not verified against actual billing.
- Halo Lab's "smart calculator" is not visible in the fetched text.
- No evidence found on conversion performance of any of these pages.

## 5. Small/mid vs large agencies; minimum page set that wins trust

### Takeaway
Smaller and mid-size shops (Monterail ~6 nav items, Stormotion, Railsware, thoughtbot) use few, deep pages with named client proof, founder or team voice, and a distinctive authority asset (handbook, framework, podcast, open source). Large vendors (Itransition, Appinventiv, STX Next, Netguru, Intellectsoft) use mega-menus, 30+ industries, hundreds of service/tech URLs, and compliance/awards pages.

### Cited Findings
- Nav size: Monterail 6 top items with no dropdowns ([monterail.com](https://www.monterail.com)); Stormotion 4 plus language switch ([stormotion.io](https://stormotion.io)); thoughtbot 6 ([thoughtbot.com](https://thoughtbot.com)) vs Appinventiv ~30 industry links and 5 service groups ([appinventiv.com](https://appinventiv.com)), STX Next 8 service groups and 14 solution pages ([stxnext.com](https://www.stxnext.com)), Toptal hundreds of skill pages ([toptal.com](https://www.toptal.com)).
- Authority assets among smaller firms: Railsware Handbook, BRIDGeS framework, products (Mailtrap, Coupler.io) ([railsware.com](https://railsware.com)); thoughtbot Playbook, books, podcasts, open source ([thoughtbot.com](https://thoughtbot.com)); Monterail podcast and open source; Halo Lab open source and AI toolkit.
- Trust modules found: client testimonials page (Netguru /testimonials, Appinventiv /clients-testimonials/), Clutch/awards badges (Netguru footer, Ramotion /awards-recognition/ and /reviews/), certificates (Netguru B-Corp, TÜV NORD), named logos in nav (Railsware dropdown lists Calendly and Tradezella), headline stats (Netguru NPS 73, 2,500+ projects).
- Directory presence matters: Ramotion footer links to Clutch; Netguru footer links Clutch — [ramotion.com](https://www.ramotion.com), [netguru.com](https://www.netguru.com).
- Generic guidance (low authority, vendor blogs): core pages are home, about, services, blog/resources, contact, plus testimonials/awards; security credentials such as ISO 27001, SOC 2, GDPR called out — [pangea.ai](https://pangea.ai/resources/how-to-choose-the-right-development-agency-in-2025), [varenyaz.com](https://varenyaz.com/essential-pages-for-service-based-websites-a-comprehensive-guide/).

### Inferences (labelled as such; derived from sections 1-4)
- Candidate minimum trust set for a 10-50 person B2B software/product agency: Home; 4-6 service pages (including one engagement/team-extension page); 3-6 industry or specialism pages only if real proof exists; 6-12 case studies with named client, problem, approach, quantified result, quote, stack and team; How we work (process + engagement models); About with named team; Security/privacy page; Careers; Contact/booking with budget range select; Blog or one authority asset; and legal pages.
- Highest-leverage differentiators that most competitors skip: a public pricing/"how budgets work" page with a stated minimum and example ranges (Halo Lab pattern), a security/trust page (Appinventiv, Ramotion trust portal), a result-first case-study index with metric-led cards (thoughtbot) and filter facets (Netguru), and a named process/handbook (Railsware, 10Clouds).
- Gulf/Arabic-specific agency patterns were not covered in this research (only Appinventiv, Railsware's Dubai office, Netguru's GCC order metric touch the region).

### Gaps
- No verified data on which page types drive conversions; no analytics-backed teardown from 2025-26 found.
- Clutch/GoodFirms profile pages and design-gallery analyses were not opened.
- Awwwards Metalab site case study exists ([awwwards.com/metalab-com-case-study.html](https://awwwards.com/metalab-com-case-study.html), seen in search results only, not opened).
