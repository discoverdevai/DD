# Gulf (Saudi first, Kuwait, UAE) and Arabic specifics for a software development company website

Scope note: research budget was about 15 tool calls. Several Saudi sites (elm.sa, thiqah.sa) failed to load (certificate errors), and no Kuwait or UAE agency sites were opened. Only 5 agency/vendor sites were read in full or in part (Mozn, Tamkeen, Itransition, Appinventiv KSA, Creole Studios KSA). The brief asked for 10+; this is a gap.

## 1. What do Saudi and Gulf agencies and software houses publish (page types, AR/EN parity, proof, certifications)?

### Takeaway
Saudi-facing vendors publish a dedicated "software development in Saudi Arabia" landing page in both languages (separate URL trees), lead proof with certifications, compliance frameworks and named integrations, and answer local-presence doubts with a declared local partner or claimed office. Self-reported proof dominates; a foreign firm's most credible differentiator is honest specificity.

### Cited Findings
- Mozn (Saudi AI/fintech): main nav is Solutions, Industries, AI Deployment, Why MOZN (Our Story, Careers, Partners, Why Trust Us), Knowledge Hub; CTAs "Request Demo" and "Get in Touch"; footer shows an ISO 27001 badge and three "Compliant with GCC AI Requirements" badges; Trust Center hosted externally; Arabic at /ar; proof = "Trusted by" logo row, analyst logo grid, awards (Datos Impact Award 2026), partner announcements — [Mozn](https://www.mozn.ai/)
- Tamkeen Technologies (Saudi): nav Company, What We Offer, Cloud, Partners, Media Center, Careers, Contact, with Arabic toggle; links two Digital Government Authority credentials (Qualification Certificate, Digital Trust Certificate); "Trusted by over +150 clients"; news feed with LEAP 2026 participation and MoUs with Fortra and Fortinet — [Tamkeen](https://www.tamkeentech.sa/)
- Itransition (international, no Gulf page found on its homepage): service pages, industry pages, case studies under /portfolio/, insights, /lp/ landing pages; proof = vendor partnerships (Microsoft since 2008, AWS, Salesforce, Odoo), headline metrics (founded 1998, 3,000+ engineers, 800+ clients), and per-case numeric results. No language options shown — [Itransition](https://www.itransition.com/)
- Appinventiv KSA (international, India-origin): country landing page at /sa/ with Arabic at /ar/; claims Arabic-first and RTL-native delivery including Hijri dates and Arabic QA; lists Saudi frameworks (PDPL, SDAIA, NCA, SAMA), international standards (ISO 27001/27017/27018/27701, SOC 2 Type II, OWASP) and local integrations (Nafath, Absher, Mada, Sadad, ZATCA e-invoicing); case studies include a Qatar government app presented on the Saudi page; CTAs "consultation", "compliance assistance request". All self-reported — [Appinventiv KSA](https://appinventiv.com/sa/software-development-company-in-saudi-arabia/)
- Creole Studios KSA Arabic page: H1 "شركة برمجيات في السعودية" (Software company in Saudi Arabia); CTAs "الحصول على استشارة خبراء" (Get expert consultation), "احصل على التقدير" (Get the estimate), "طلب استشارة" (Request consultation), "اتصل بنا" (Contact us); 15-block structure: hero, rating badges (Google, Upwork, Clutch, GoodFirms), cities (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam), partner section, client logos, services, sectors, portfolio, stats, testimonials, cost-estimate CTA, 8-question FAQ, closing CTA. No office address or certifications listed; local presence rests on a named partner (Surnouf Technology) offering 24/7 local support — [Creole Studios KSA (ar)](https://www.creolestudios.com/ksa/ar/)
- Creole's FAQ questions (Arabic with meaning): "هل تقومون بتطوير أنظمة مؤسسية مخصصة في السعودية؟" (Do you develop custom enterprise systems in Saudi Arabia?); "ما هي القطاعات التي تخدمونها في السعودية؟" (Which sectors do you serve in Saudi Arabia?) — same source
- Another Riyadh-targeted vendor advertises ISO 27001:2022 while delivering remotely from an India-based team; listicle "best of Riyadh" pages are repetitive vendor-sponsored content — [search results incl. CloudHouse](https://cloudhousetechnologies.com/services/mobile-app-development-company-riyadh)
- Search for Arabic "software company Saudi" returns Creole's /ksa/ar/ page and local firms (e.g. Moltaqa Software); Arabic page URL patterns seen: /ksa/ar/ (Creole), /ar/ (Appinventiv, Mozn)

### Inferences
- Common page set to mirror: country landing page ("Software development in Saudi Arabia"), services, sectors, case studies, trust/compliance, partners, about, contact, FAQ. A Trust/Why-Trust-Us page (Mozn) is a pattern worth copying.
- Separate Arabic URL tree (/ar/) with its own landing page is the norm; Arabic is not a machine-translated mirror on the stronger sites.
- Creole's model (named local partner, no fake office) is the closest analogue to DiscoverDev's situation.
- Proof lists that cite many standards without certificate numbers are the market default; a smaller but verifiable list would stand out.

### Gaps
- Elm, Thiqah, Seven Technologies, Kuwait and UAE agency sites could not be opened (certificate errors / redirect to a domain-for-sale page). No Kuwait or UAE page-type findings.
- Could not verify Arabic/English parity on Mozn or Tamkeen (only English content was returned).
- Appinventiv page was only read to character 100,000 of 131,213 (FAQ section not captured).

## 2. Buyer expectations: local presence/CR, vendor registration, PDPL, NCA, Arabic, WhatsApp, ZATCA, Nafath, Mada, local contracting

### Takeaway
Government and PIF-linked buyers effectively require a Saudi legal entity (CR, VAT, bank, Etimad account) and push local content; private buyers are more flexible but increasingly prefer local presence. A foreign firm should sell to private/startup buyers directly and route anything government-adjacent through a local contracting partner. PDPL applies and requires transfer safeguards.

### Cited Findings
- Etimad bidding requires a commercial registration, VAT registration, GOSI, Zakat certificate, bank account and an authorised contact (with Absher account per one guide) — [SetupInSaudi on Etimad](https://www.setupinsaudi.com/en/news/announcements/how-to-use-saudi-arabia-s-etimad-to-bid-on-governmental-contracts)
- Regional headquarters rule: state entities barred from contracting with foreign firms whose regional HQ is outside Saudi Arabia; exceptions via Etimad requested before tender; non-RHQ offers accepted only if at most one technically compliant bid, or among the best technically and at least 25% cheaper than second best; projects of SAR 1 million or less exempt per Gulf News — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2633989/), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fnew-rule-saudi-arabia-reverses-restriction-allows-foreign-companies-in-government-projects-1.500451195)
- One consultancy says for large semi-government entities and PIF-related projects local incorporation is explicitly required to sign contracts; sources disagree on strictness for private buyers — [SetupInSaudi](https://www.setupinsaudi.com/news/announcements/winning-government-contracts-in-saudi-what-foreign-companies-need-to-know)
- Local content: LCGPA sets local content requirements per contract, tracks contractor use, keeps a mandatory list of locally sourced products/services; local content share in procurement reached 47% in 2025 vs a 45% target — [Saudipedia on LCGPA](https://saudipedia.com/en/local-content-and-government-procurement-authority), [search summary via dgagroup](https://dgagroup.com/insight/asg-analysis-new-saudi-procurement-regulations/)
- PDPL: amended by Royal Decree M/148 (27 March 2023); effective 14 September 2023 with enforcement from September 2024; approval requirement for transfers removed, replaced by "appropriate level of protection" abroad; SDAIA issued standard contractual clauses (must be incorporated even if EU SCCs used) and BCR guidance — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/saudi-arabia-new-amendments-have-been-introduced-to-the-personal-data-protection-law), [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/fr/insights/2024/09/saudi-arabia-s-personal-data-protection-law-become), [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2023/data-protection/saudi-arabia-issues-amendments-to-pdpl/)
- ZATCA Phase 2 e-invoicing (Fatoora) runs in waves for Saudi VAT-registered businesses: wave 24 deadline 30 June 2026; wave 25 starts 1 February 2027; requires integration, XML invoices, cryptographic stamp, QR — [EY wave 25](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1705-saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration), [ClearTax wave 24](https://www.cleartax.com/sa/zatca-wave24-einvoicing-in-saudi-arabia); sources differ on wave 24 dates
- Vendors list Nafath, Absher, Mada, Sadad, ZATCA integration, NCA and SAMA alignment as expected capability keywords — [Appinventiv KSA](https://appinventiv.com/sa/software-development-company-in-saudi-arabia/)
- Advice for buyers: ask vendors for certificate numbers and certifier, NCA control assessments (ECC/CCC), Saudi references, and whether the team is in-Kingdom — [search summary](https://cloudhousetechnologies.com/services/mobile-app-development-company-riyadh)

### Inferences
- DiscoverDev can honestly claim PDPL-aware engineering and integration experience only if proven in 09-proof; "NCA compliant" must not be claimed (NCA controls apply to buyer entities and certified providers).
- ZATCA applies to the Saudi invoicing entity: a foreign firm invoicing from Egypt is generally not on the Fatoora waves, but a contracting partner is. This is an inference; needs legal confirmation.
- The "local contracting partner" model should be stated plainly on a "How we work in Saudi Arabia" page, with the partner named only with consent.

### Gaps
- No source found on WhatsApp as a B2B channel in KSA (widely assumed, not cited here), Kuwait or UAE buyer rules, Saudi vendor-registration alternatives for foreign firms beyond Etimad, or ZATCA rules for non-resident suppliers.
- NCA: no primary NCA page was reached; no verified statement on what NCA requires of IT vendors.
- Monsha'at/accelerator ties and Wamda/MAGNiTT coverage not researched.
- elm.sa, sdaia.gov.sa fetches failed; PDPL facts come from law-firm summaries.

## 3. Arabic SEO (keywords, dialect, hreflang, engines, title/meta lengths)

### Takeaway
Google supports language-only hreflang (ar); region codes (ar-SA) are only worth adding when content truly differs by country. Use one MSA-based Arabic version first, with Saudi/Gulf vocabulary, and add ar-SA/ar-KW only for distinct localized pages (such as a Saudi landing page).

### Cited Findings
- Google: codes are language plus optional region; "de: German language content, independent of region" is valid; region can't be used alone; x-default is the fallback and works best on language-selector pages — [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)
- "ar" serves a generic Arabic page; country codes such as ar-eg are optional; large brands (Apple, Canva) run separate sites per Arabic market — [Argos Multilingual](https://www.argosmultilingual.com/blog/arabic-seo)
- MSA is the standardized baseline; dialect and slang considered mainly for B2C campaigns aimed at young users in one country — same source
- Arabic keyword tools show far lower volumes (rarely thousands); practitioners pick keywords around volume 10 and lean on SERP analysis and People Also Ask; 90-95% of Arab-world searches are on mobile (self-reported by the agency); YouTube dominates many Arabic SERPs — same source
- Vendor claim: dialect regions (ar-SA, ar-EG, ar-AE) may rank differently even with identical content — [MotaWord](https://3h.motaword.com/languages/arabic-website-localization) (low-quality source)
- Observed practice: a Saudi page and an Arabic page exist as separate URL trees (/sa/ + /ar/ at Appinventiv; /ksa/ar/ at Creole) — see section 1

### Inferences
- Recommended: hreflang en + ar (+ x-default), and optionally en-SA/ar-SA on a Saudi landing page pair only. Do not tag ar-KW/ar-AE without distinct content.
- Mixed Arabic/English queries (e.g. "تطوير تطبيقات mobile app", "شركة software") are likely common in Gulf B2B but I found no cited data; treat as an assumption.

### Gaps
- No cited data on Arabic title/meta length limits (Google truncates by pixel width; Arabic glyph widths differ), how Google/AI engines treat Arabic content, Gulf vs Egyptian dialect in SEO, or Arabizi queries.

## 4. Arabic UX and writing (RTL, fonts, numerals, tone, CTAs)

### Takeaway
Use logical CSS, mirror directional icons, set a purpose-built Arabic font with extra line-height and zero letter-spacing, keep phone numbers/Latin terms LTR. Numeral choice is not settled in the sources; test with Saudi reviewers.

### Cited Findings
- dir="rtl" does not mirror directional icons (arrows, carousels, progress bars); logical properties such as margin-inline-start avoid separate stylesheets; Latin fonts lack Arabic glyphs causing fallback; Arabic needs more line-height and letter-spacing must stay zero to preserve joins — [Saudisoft RTL checklist](https://localization.saudisoft.com/?p=38918)
- Numbers and phone numbers stay LTR inside RTL text; Hindu-Arabic digits are in everyday use alongside Western digits — [freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/designing-for-the-arab-user-basic-arabic-ux-for-business-6ff29d4c7c60/), [Greenpeace RTL doc](https://jira.greenpeace.org/secure/attachment/27098/Right-to-Left%20_%20Overall%20design%20principles.pdf)
- Single informal blog claims Saudi/UAE/Egypt expect Eastern digits and Maghreb Western — unverified — [Element8](https://www.element8.ae/blogs/arabic-ux-rtl-bilingual-seo-uae)
- Treating Arabic as translation loses trust; bilingual Gulf sites often use English for business, Arabic as official, so design each version for its audience — same Element8 / search summary
- Working Arabic CTAs from a live Saudi-facing agency: "طلب استشارة" (Request consultation), "احصل على التقدير" (Get the estimate), "اتصل بنا" (Contact us) — [Creole Studios KSA](https://www.creolestudios.com/ksa/ar/)
- Appinventiv advertises Hijri dates and Arabic QA as selling points — [Appinventiv KSA](https://appinventiv.com/sa/software-development-company-in-saudi-arabia/)

### Inferences
- Project rule "CSS logical properties only" matches best practice. Add: Arabic font stack (e.g. IBM Plex Sans Arabic or Noto Sans Arabic; not verified in sources), Western digits by default is the safer technical choice for B2B with mixed Latin tech terms, pending a native reviewer.
- Egyptian authors writing Saudi-facing copy should stick to formal MSA and avoid Egyptian colloquialisms; no source gives Saudi B2B tone rules.

### Gaps
- No cited source on B2B tone norms in Saudi, honorifics, or CTA conversion data; no verified font recommendations.

## 5. Trust signals and honest presentation for a foreign firm without a local office

### Takeaway
Gulf vendors lead with government credentials, ISO, named partners and Saudi references. A firm with none of these should state its model plainly: where it is based, who the local contracting partner is, what is delivered remotely, and what it does not claim.

### Cited Findings
- Credible local trust markers seen: DGA Qualification and Digital Trust certificates (Tamkeen), ISO 27001 and GCC AI badges (Mozn), analyst/award logos (Mozn), exhibition presence (LEAP 2026) and MoUs (Tamkeen) — see section 1
- Creole Studios discloses a local partner (Surnouf Technology) instead of an office — [Creole Studios KSA](https://www.creolestudios.com/ksa/ar/)
- Buyers are advised to verify ISO certificate numbers, NCA assessments, Saudi references and in-Kingdom team presence; some Riyadh-branded vendors deliver from India — [search summary](https://cloudhousetechnologies.com/services/mobile-app-development-company-riyadh)
- Creole's "client logos" block contains placeholder images only, a pattern to avoid — Creole page

### Inferences
- Suggested honest elements: "Built in Cairo, contracted in Saudi Arabia through [partner]" page; list only verified clients (Saudi, Kuwait, US per brief) with consent; no fake RHQ, CR or ISO claims; avoid compliance badges not held; frame PDPL/ZATCA/Nafath/Mada as "we integrate with" not "we are certified".
- Government work is out of reach without the partner holding the CR/Etimad registration (see section 2).

### Gaps
- Monsha'at and accelerator relevance, Wamda/MAGNiTT/Arab News evidence, and Saudi reference-check norms were not researched.
